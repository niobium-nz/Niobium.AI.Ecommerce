# Evidence and debranding

## Evidence ledger

For each photo, record source identity, observed viewpoint, product state,
material exterior features, complete/partial silhouette, occluded regions,
marking locations, and relevant color/lighting uncertainty. Record a proposed
view-to-source mapping. This is an observation record, not a request to disclose
hidden reasoning. Keep filenames and notes outside the image itself.

Inspect every photo at useful resolution before rejecting it. Do not reject a
photo wholesale because advertising copy or a person dominates the frame. Retain
the clear product regions, especially unique latches, ports, undersides, surface
textures and attachments that appear nowhere else. Structural clarity outranks
aesthetic polish. A unique feature in one clear photo is not an outlier to erase.

Distinguish evidence problems:

| Observation | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Rear, underside or other material surface unseen | Request the specific view; never assume symmetry. |
| Incompatible port count, colorway, shape or construction | Resolve variant/SKU identity; do not merge. |
| Same object open in one source and closed in another | Preserve configuration differences; do not average them. |
| Text overlay, glare, hand or prop hides structure | Find a source that reveals it; otherwise ask for an unobscured photo. |
| Scene object may be an attachment | Resolve component identity using other photos or user clarification. |
| Conflicting lighting casts | Preserve supported material/color appearance; ask when color remains materially ambiguous. |
| Same product appears more than once in a board | Treat as repeated views of one SKU, not evidence of quantity supplied. |

Do not claim knowledge of internals, unseen sides, scale in physical units, or
material chemistry from appearance alone. Do not use outside lookalikes or
product-category priors to fill gaps. Photos need not be novel to qualify, and
novelty is not a fact the skill must verify by market research.

## Mandatory cosmetic debranding

The user requires removal of all logos and branding physically present on the
product. Treat this as the sole deliberate identity-related departure from the
photographed appearance. Do not make debranding optional in this skill.

Remove brand names, wordmarks, trademarks and brand symbols, including printing,
embroidered marks, engraved marks, raised logo relief and branding on actual
product badges. Do not introduce a new name, placeholder logo or replacement mark.
Remove photographic promotional overlays and watermarks as visual noise as well.

Preserve actual product colors even when the manufacturer uses them for brand
identity. Preserve functional or safety text, calibrated scale marks, control
icons, seams, screws, apertures, textures, functional relief and construction.
Preserve other supported non-brand surface printing and patterns as part of actual
appearance; do not classify all text as branding. Do not manufacture illegible
characters. Ask for clearer evidence when unclear
markings materially affect usability or visual identification.

## Local editing boundary

Prefer a masked edit confined to the mark. For flat printing on an otherwise
clearly observed continuous surface, use surrounding visible material for a
minimal texture-matched cosmetic fill. For a logo on a raised badge, preserve the
badge carrier, size, fasteners and attachment; remove only brand identity. For
embossing/engraving, remove only the brand-shaped relief when the underlying
surface and non-brand geometry are sufficiently evidenced.

This limited cosmetic fill is expressly authorized debranding, not proof of a
photographed unbranded surface. Never use it to reconstruct a hidden latch, an
unknown edge, an occluded control or a missing side. Prefer another clear source
for structural details; do not transplant perspective-incompatible pixels and
pretend they align naturally.

If a logo is itself the housing silhouette, a load-bearing feature, a functional
control or inseparable from essential instructions, request a decision or better
source rather than deleting necessary geometry. If an adhesive label conceals an
unknown seam or aperture, request an unobscured/unbranded photo rather than
inventing the substrate. Do not return a branded board as if debranding succeeded.

When branding and functional information share a label, remove the brand portion
only and preserve supported functional content. Retain the label/carrier shape
when it is a physical part. Remove loose retail packaging; do not mistake a
functional product container or permanent cover for packaging to be deleted.

## Source-preserving production

Prefer cutouts of complete observed views and mask-based retouch. Preserve camera
perspective, natural surface variation, manufacturing details and non-brand
irregularities. Do not beautify the product into an idealized version. Do not
stretch, reverse, warp or duplicate components to make views match.

A generative editor may perform constrained debranding/cleanup with the actual
sources. It may not invent structure or unseen views. Compare its result with
sources rather than assuming that reference conditioning preserved every detail.
If reliable preservation is unavailable, stop rather than substituting a text-only
redraw. Do not create a 3D model or simulated turntable to supply missing evidence.

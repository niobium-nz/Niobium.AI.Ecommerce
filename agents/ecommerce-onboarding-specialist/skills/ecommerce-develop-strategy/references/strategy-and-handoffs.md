# Strategy and handoffs

## Product framing

Infer the job-to-be-done, core problem, usage occasion, visible-demo opportunity
and objections from actual identity and supplied material. Label inferred use cases
as hypotheses. Do not turn competitor marketing into verified performance, certified
construction or customer outcomes. Do not include critiques of competitors.

Generate 5 to 10 original product-name concepts with a primary choice and rationale.
Do not copy competitor branding or near-clone spelling. Do not claim availability
or trademark clearance. Repeat the same primary identity in every segment snapshot.
Angle-specific name alternatives may vary, but explain them as concepts for testing,
not multiple simultaneously established brands.

## Segment architecture

Normally create 3 to 5 prioritized, non-duplicative segments and order by expected
profit potential. Use 1 to 5 integers for overall priority, profit potential,
trigger intensity, creative clarity and funnel fit. Support ratings with concrete
usage/economic reasoning; they are hypotheses, not validated performance scores.
Differentiate when justified and explain ties rather than manufacturing precision.
Explain departures from the usual segment count in the existing rationale fields.

For each segment, complete the shared context, rating, summary, angle matrix and
landing-page adaptation. Repeat the primary name, offer stack, currency/price points,
shipping reality, authorized guarantee/refund framing and page role. Do not rely on
"same as above". State need, purchase moment, emotional driver, objections and why
the order economics could work for that segment.

Prefer motivations, situations and product use cases over unsupported demographic
assumptions. Do not recommend discriminatory targeting, exploitation of vulnerable
groups, or sensitive-personal-attribute accusations in copy. A target-market country
is not evidence of individual traits or a license to invent cultural stereotypes.

## Every angle

Complete each declared field, not just the prose outline: angle number and name,
trigger, original name options, chosen angle name with rationale, short product code,
primary/secondary/accent website colors with rationale, core promise, message
territory, conversion hypothesis, objections, proof tasks, CTA or justified ROAS,
and the creative-handoff object. Use explicit proposed colors such as hex codes
for implementability, and check readable foreground/background combinations before
production without claiming unperformed accessibility testing.

Give the angle a distinct purchase reason or moment. Do not count synonyms as
new strategies. Express conversion expectations as hypotheses and identify the
proof needed to support the promise. Use a CTA alone when a ROAS target would
lack a consistent economic basis. Do not output final ad files or launch campaigns.

## Creative handoffs and planned assets

Specify static directions, short-form video or creator-style demonstration
directions, first-frame direction, copy-hook and headline territories, visual proof
moments and landing-page continuity. Distinguish a creator demonstration to produce
from an existing customer testimonial. Do not invent reviews, transformations,
numerical performance, certifications or expert endorsements.

Describe assets in enough detail for the next agent: subject, situation, composition,
sequence, what is visibly demonstrated, crop/readability needs, text placement,
substantiation dependency, and connection to the offer/angle. Avoid instructions
that promise untested performance. Mark every asset as planned, not generated.

Define one `lp-...-NN` ID per distinct asset in the page asset plan. Use consistent
references plus short descriptions in segment and angle fields, so each segment
still makes sense independently. When a segment-specific asset differs materially,
include its own ID and full description in the central asset plan rather than
reusing an unrelated ID. Do not require hashes, UUID tools or code execution.

## Mobile-first landing page

Keep the explicit source output structure: page role, section order, detailed asset
plans, proof strategy without reviews, shipping/guarantee guidance, FAQ priorities,
segment/angle alignment and mobile UX. Ensure the same sections appear in the order
and their asset plans. Give each section a clear decision-making job.

Align the hero and early demonstration with the ad promise, then make offer math,
compatibility, shipping and objections easy to scan. Cover thumb reach, CTA placement,
mobile crops, readable text, page-speed sensitivity and continuity. Do not declare
that a design has been produced, tested or measured. Detail adaptations rather than
rebuilding unrelated pages for superficially different segments.

## Trust and operational reality

Treat regulatory compliance as the business's responsibility, not a service this
skill certifies. Preserve the source safety constraints: no fake proof, fake urgency,
hidden delivery timelines, unsupported guarantees or unsubstantiated regulated
claims. Prefer visible demonstrations of convenience, usability and product behavior.

Use the stated overseas 1 to 2 week fulfillment assumption visibly, pending merchant
confirmation. Do not offer urgent-delivery promises that conflict with it. Keep the
zero-return-shipping assumption internal. Proposed guarantees require merchant
approval and operational support before publishing; competitor terms do not provide
that authorization. An approved policy is not present merely because a competitor
used it. Present optional policy recommendations as proposals, not live promises.
